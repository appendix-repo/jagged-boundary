PAIR 1→2 | 1 | Going from fixing spelling to fixing grammar and punctuation, with each suggestion accepted or rejected, is still mechanical surface correction under the author's control.
PAIR 2→3 | 1 | Moving from grammar correction to automated citation formatting changes only which routine mechanical task the software handles.
PAIR 3→4 | 1 | Checking reference details against databases instead of just formatting them is still bibliographic housekeeping with no effect on content.
PAIR 4→5 | 1 | Software plotting figures from data the author produced is standard practice and adds no intellectual contribution.
PAIR 5→6 | 1 | Running simulations of a model the author specified, rather than plotting existing output, leaves the model and its design with the author.
PAIR 6→7 | 1 | Swapping simulation software for a statistical package to run the reported analyses is a change of tool, not of who does the work.
PAIR 7→8 | 2 | This is the first use of an LLM, though only to flag code errors the author fixes, so only a few would object, mainly on policy or confidentiality grounds.
PAIR 8→9 | 2 | The LLM now sees the manuscript prose rather than the code, but it still only flags errors and the author makes every correction.
PAIR 9→10 | 2 | The LLM now makes the corrections itself instead of flagging them, which removes per-item author review, though the corrections are still only spelling and grammar.
PAIR 10→11 | 3 | The LLM moves from fixing correctness to proposing stylistic wording, which is where some researchers draw the line on machine-generated language.
PAIR 11→12 | 2 | Suggesting wordings for a whole sentence rather than a phrase is a small increase in scope, and the author still picks among options.
PAIR 12→13 | 2 | The LLM now redrafts the sentence directly instead of offering options to choose from, a modest loss of author selection with the content unchanged.
PAIR 13→14 | 2 | Moving from a sentence to a paragraph enlarges the amount of machine-written text, but the content is still the author's.
PAIR 14→15 | 3 | Reordering and restating the points to clarify the argument, rather than just improving clarity, lets the LLM shape how the reasoning is presented.
PAIR 15→15b | 4 | The LLM now turns notes into the finished prose instead of revising prose the author wrote, which is where many researchers draw the authorship line.
PAIR 15b→16 | 2 | Composing a paragraph from notes that list each point differs little from turning ordered notes into prose.
PAIR 16→17 | 3 | Composing a whole section rather than a single paragraph greatly increases the share of the paper's text the LLM writes, even from a detailed outline.
PAIR 17→17b | 3 | Working from a few bullet points rather than a detailed outline shifts much of the structure and development of the introduction to the LLM.
PAIR 17b→18 | 4 | Leaving the framing of the paper's contribution to the model hands over a core intellectual judgment, not just the prose.
PAIR 18→19 | 3 | Writing the literature review means the LLM selects and characterizes the scholarship, which raises concerns about scholarly judgment and fabricated sources.
PAIR 19→19b | 3 | The LLM now suggests hypotheses, the substantive content of the research rather than its prose, even though the author chooses and refines them.
PAIR 19b→20 | 3 | Having the LLM derive the hypotheses instead of offering options to choose from moves the logical derivation itself to the model.
PAIR 20→21 | 3 | Drafting the theoretical argument itself, even from the author's detailed outline, delegates the core reasoning of the paper.
PAIR 21→21b | 4 | Working from a one-paragraph statement of the idea rather than a detailed outline leaves most of the theoretical argument's development to the LLM.
PAIR 21b→22 | 5 | Without the author's own statement of the central idea, the paper's main intellectual contribution originates with the model.
PAIR 22→23 | 2 | Once the central argument is generated by the LLM, having it generate the hypotheses as well is a small further step.
PAIR 23→24 | 2 | An agent building the formal model of the author's own theory is, if anything, no more delegation than LLM-generated theory, so the agent framing matters little here.
PAIR 24→25 | 2 | Specifying the empirical tests instead of the formal model shifts the task to a different research component, with a similar degree of delegation.
PAIR 25→26 | 3 | The agent now runs the analyses and writes up the results, and the author only checks them rather than refining them, which delegates both execution and reporting.
PAIR 26→27a | 1 | Checking the author's own proofs is a verification role, which involves less delegation than the previous item.
PAIR 27a→27b | 3 | Generating the proofs rather than checking them hands the mathematical work behind the results to the agent.
PAIR 27b→28 | 3 | Specifying extensions has the agent produce new propositions and boundary conditions rather than proofs of theorems the author stated.
PAIR 28→28b | 3 | Developing the theory from the author's core mechanism, rather than extending an existing theory, makes the agent responsible for the theory's construction.
PAIR 28b→29 | 4 | Starting from only a brief statement of the phenomenon, with no mechanism from the author, means the theory's central idea comes from the agent.
PAIR 29→30 | 3 | Writing the entire paper (theory, analysis and prose) rather than just the theory delegates all the remaining components, though substantial author revision remains.
PAIR 30→31 | 4 | Dropping the substantial revision means the author only approves and submits the agent's paper, which many would see as decisive for authorship.
